\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In machine-text detection, burstiness is the variation of perplexity across a
document; in everyday use it is the variation of sentence length, whose obvious
statistic, the variance of sentence length, grows with mean sentence length. We
give the sentence-length reading a scale-free definition: sentence boundaries as a point
process in word-time, scored with the Goh--Barab\'asi burstiness coefficient under the
Kim--Jo finite-$n$ correction, bounded and with no model in the loop. The
protocol is paired evaluation on $200$ era-, topic-, and register-matched
Wikinews articles dated 2016--2021: five prompting modes, nine models, three
samples per headline and mode, uniform normalization of generated text before
scoring. Scored raw, markdown scaffolding manufactures
dispersion: on the audited model it produces an equivalence verdict that normalization reverses
(instruction-mode $\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.d}$ scored raw,
\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.eqphrase}; $\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.d}$ on normalized text,
significantly different, $\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.p}$, and equivalent only from
$m^{*}=\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.mstar}$ upward), and its uninstructed gap grows from $\val{qwenraw.Bn.control.d}$ to
$\val{qwen.Bn.control.d}$. Uninstructed, \val{ctrl.flat.w} of the nine models write significantly flatter
than the human articles, a median paired gap of $\val{ctrl.d.min}$ to $\val{ctrl.d.max}$ in $B_n$. Of the
forty-five model-by-mode cells, \val{census.flat.w} land significantly
flatter than the human articles, \val{census.ns.w} show no significant
difference, and \val{census.past.w} land significantly more dispersed: machine
flatness is a strong central tendency that the prompts tested move but do not
reliably close. Under the same instruction, \val{inst.flat.w} models remain
significantly flatter than the human articles, \val{inst.ns.w} show no
significant difference and \val{inst.past.w} writes significantly more dispersed
text; \val{inst.eq05.w} of the nine are equivalent within $\pm0.05$ and \val{inst.eq08.w} within
$\pm0.08$, \val{inst.eq08.sig.w} of them while differing significantly (\val{inst.eq03.w} within $\pm0.03$), and for \val{con.widen.phrase} among the
nine the instruction makes the text significantly flatter than its own
uninstructed output. Few-shot exemplars of typical human news prose produce
text significantly flatter than the instruction alone in \val{con.fslt.phrase}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Burstiness is one of the signals most often cited to separate human writing
from machine writing~\citep{arxiv2024detecting}, and the word carries two
readings. In detector practice it means the variation of perplexity across a
document, part of the sense GPTZero gave it~\citep{gptzero2023burstiness}. In everyday
use it means the variation of sentence length, the mix of long and short
sentences that gives prose its rhythm and that language models flatten toward a
middle. The obvious statistic for that reading, the variance or standard
deviation of sentence length, grows with mean sentence length, so uniformly long
sentences score burstier than varied short ones. This paper is about the second
reading.

Sentence-length dispersion has been a stylometric variable since
\citet{yule1939sentence} and \citet{williams1940note}, but its natural statistics, the
variance and the standard deviation of sentence length, grow with the mean, and
the scale-free ratio \citet{onan2025you} use has a range that shrinks in short
documents. The aim of this paper is to give the sentence-length
reading a statistic that is both scale-free and range-corrected, and to use it to
benchmark five prompting techniques.

\paragraph{A definition.}
We treat sentence boundaries as a point process in word-time and score the
sentence-length sequence with the Goh--Barab\'asi burstiness
coefficient~\citep{goh2008burstiness} under the Kim--Jo finite-$n$
correction~\citep{kim2016measuring}, written $B_n$. It is bounded in
$[-1, 1]$, needs no reference model, and is a function of the coefficient of
variation and the sentence count alone, so it is scale-free: scaling every
sentence length leaves it unchanged, and dispersion is measured relative to
typical sentence length instead of conflated with it. Alongside $B_n$ we report a
Gini robustness check~\citep{gini1912variabilit}, Miller--Madow-corrected
punctuation entropy as a separate segmentation axis, and a memory coefficient
$M$ for lag-1 order; surprisal, the strong but model-relative detector
signal, stays deliberately outside the construct. Because a metric that
becomes a target invites gaming, we add a Goodhart guard, a screen that flags
degenerate constructions that inflate any dispersion score, for any use that
selects text by the score; a quality gate in the sense of
\citet{arxiv2025evaluating} is the second condition such a use would need.

\paragraph{A benchmark of five prompting techniques.}
We evaluate control (a bare brief), instruction, few-shot human
exemplars~\citep{brown2020language, min2022rethinking}, an outline-then-write
plan mode~\citep{wang2023plan}, and a draft-critique-rewrite self-refine
loop~\citep{madaan2023self} against $200$ English Wikinews
articles~\citep{wikinews2021wikinews} dated 2016--2021, with era, topic, and
register held fixed. Each generation is length-matched to its paired article,
and every cell is sampled three times. Generated text is uniformly
normalized before scoring, because markdown scaffolding and echoed headlines
in raw output manufacture dispersion the human baseline lacks, and on the
audited leg this choice moves the uninstructed dispersion gap from
$\val{qwenraw.Bn.control.d}$ to $\val{qwen.Bn.control.d}$.
The identical design runs across nine models, spanning open-weight
checkpoints (Qwen3.6-27B, Gemma 3 27B), API-served models (DeepSeek V4 Pro,
GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.6, Grok 4.3), and closed frontier systems (Grok 4.5,
Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.8).

\paragraph{The aim problem.}
Prompting changes sentence-length dispersion, and the landing depends on the
model. Uninstructed, \val{ctrl.flat.w} of the nine models write
significantly flatter than the human articles, with control deficits
$\val{ctrl.d.min}$ to $\val{ctrl.d.max}$; the three largest belong to Gemini 3.1 Pro,
GLM-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.8. Under the identical instruction, \val{inst.flat.w}
remain significantly flatter, \val{inst.ns.w} show no significant difference and
\val{inst.past.w} writes significantly more dispersed text; \val{inst.eq05.w} of
the nine are equivalent within $\pm0.05$ and \val{inst.eq08.w} within $\pm0.08$,
\val{inst.eq08.sig.w} of them while differing significantly (\val{inst.eq03.w} within $\pm0.03$), and
for \val{con.widen.phrase} among the nine the instruction makes the text
significantly flatter than its own uninstructed output; a model's uninstructed
gap is a weak guide to where it lands. Few-shot exemplars of typical human news
prose produce text significantly flatter than the instruction alone in
\val{con.fslt.phrase}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A reproducible definition of sentence-length burstiness that is
model-free and scale-free: the Goh--Barab\'asi coefficient under the Kim--Jo
finite-$n$ correction, with companions (Gini check, segmentation-diversity
axis, memory coefficient; surprisal kept outside) and a Goodhart guard proposed
for any use that selects text by the score (Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}).
\item A paired protocol on era-, topic-, and register-matched news articles:
uniform normalization before scoring, seed-averaging that avoids
pseudo-replication, within-model multiplicity correction, equivalence only as a
CI inside pre-specified margins, a contamination probe,
and a moderation-refusal audit (Sections~\ref{sec:corpus}--\ref{sec:ninefam} and~\ref{sec:limitations}).
\item The aim problem across nine models, five modes, three samples per cell:
every mode-to-mode claim the text builds on carries a direct paired contrast
with rankings marked descriptive, and
few-shot exemplars produce text significantly flatter than the instruction
alone in \val{con.fslt.phrase} (Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Burstiness in detection}
In detector practice the term comes from GPTZero, whose original detector paired perplexity with a
burstiness scalar, the variation of writing patterns and perplexity across the
document~\citep{gptzero2023burstiness}, and which recent attribution work uses as a baseline detector~\citep{arxiv2025training}; the
survey that cites it describes burstiness as variability in style, tone and
vocabulary~\citep{arxiv2024detecting}. The sentence-length reading travels under the same name:
\citet{onan2025you} define burstiness as the variance of sentence-level perplexities and compute
it as the coefficient of variation of sentence lengths, a scale-free ratio whose range
shrinks in short documents; Section~\ref{sec:formalizing} gives the range-corrected form.
DivEye~\citep{arxiv2025diveye} tracks how unpredictability fluctuates across a text, the nearest
text-side analog to timing variation. Surveys~\citep{arxiv2024detecting} treat
perplexity-burstiness as a standard pair and document detector vulnerability to paraphrasing.

\subsection{Stylistic variation and diversity}
The stylistic-variation benchmark~\citep{arxiv2025benchmark} compares frontier models'
counterparts of human reference texts with those texts using Biber's multidimensional analysis, a
register-level comparison rather than one of sentence-length dispersion. Effective semantic diversity~\citep{arxiv2025evaluating} gates diversity on
quality; a gate of that kind is the condition we would attach to any use of $B_n$ that selects
text, so that incoherent high-variance text is not rewarded.

\section{Formalizing burstiness}\label{sec:formalizing}

We give the sentence-length reading of burstiness (Section~\ref{sec:related}) a model-free,
reproducible instrument.

\subsection{Burstiness as a point process}
Let a document $D$ be segmented into sentences with word-length sequence $L = (l_1, \dots, l_n)$. We
treat the sentence boundaries as a point process in word-time, so $L$ is a sequence of inter-event
intervals, and score it with the Goh--Barab\'asi burstiness coefficient~\citep{goh2008burstiness},
\[
B = \frac{\sigma - \mu}{\sigma + \mu},
\]
where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of $L$. $B$ is bounded in $[-1, 1]$:
$B = -1$ for a metronomic sequence, $B = 0$ for a Poisson one, and $B \to 1$ for
a maximally bursty one. Because real documents give short interval sequences, we apply the Kim--Jo
finite-$n$ correction~\citep{kim2016measuring}: with $r = \sigma / \mu$ and $n$ sentences,
\[
B_n = \frac{\sqrt{n+1}\,r - \sqrt{n-1}}{(\sqrt{n+1} - 2)\,r + \sqrt{n-1}},
\]
which reduces to $B = (r-1)/(r+1)$ as $n$ grows and, unlike $B$, spans $[-1, 1]$ at every $n$.

$B_n$ is \emph{scale-free}: it depends on the sentence lengths only through $r$ and $n$, so scaling
every length by the same factor leaves it unchanged, and it measures variation \emph{relative} to
typical sentence length rather than conflating dispersion with length. Classically, $B$ is a bounded reparametrization of the coefficient of
variation, weak alone in the stylometric literature~\citep{yule1939sentence, williams1940note}. What
is new is the corrected, model-free formulation, its paired evaluation on a matched modern corpus,
and the nine-model prompt benchmark built on it (Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}).

\subsection{The construct and its companions}
Burstiness proper is the single scale-free coefficient $B_n$. We report four companions
alongside it, none folded into the construct:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gini robustness check.} The Gini coefficient~\citep{gini1912variabilit} of the sentence
lengths is a scale-free dispersion measure that aggregates over all pairwise differences, so it is
less hostage to a single long sentence. It agrees with $B_n$ in direction on every cell of the
nine-model benchmark where either difference is significant (Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling});
agreement shows a result is not an estimator artifact.
\item \textbf{Segmentation diversity, a separate axis.} The Miller--Madow-corrected Shannon entropy of
the punctuation distribution measures how varied the segmentation symbols are, a distinct axis from
length dispersion. Typographic quotes are mapped to ASCII before the punctuation count and before
sentence splitting (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}), so smart-quote typography registers neither
as diversity nor as a missed boundary.
\item \textbf{Memory, the order term.} The Goh--Barab\'asi memory coefficient
$M$~\citep{goh2008burstiness}, the lag-1 correlation between consecutive interval lengths plus
$1/(n-1)$, which offsets the lag-1 estimator's small-sample bias of about $-1/(n-1)$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:refimpl}), is the order information a dispersion statistic discards. In Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}
\val{fs.Mover.w} models' few-shot legs overshoot the human articles on memory while undershooting
on dispersion.
\item \textbf{Predictability, kept outside.} Mean per-token surprisal and the mean absolute
consecutive difference, under a fixed reference model, form the strong but model-relative detector
signal. The definition places them \emph{outside} the construct: they are a first-moment quantity, whereas burstiness is a second moment of a derived sequence. The benchmark scores the four model-free dimensions only and reports no surprisal values.
\end{itemize}
We compute raw sentence-length variance $\mathrm{var}(L)$ only as a diagnostic; it is confounded with
absolute length by construction, the central defect of the colloquial statistic.
Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail} scores the benchmark with it beside $B_n$ (Table~\ref{tab:variance}),
and its verdicts follow length as well as dispersion.

\subsection{Anti-gaming screens and reproducibility}
A metric that becomes a selection score invites gaming. Degenerate texts can reach a
high-dispersion score (one giant sentence among fragments, mechanical alternation, punctuation spam).
A Goodhart guard flags these; the full flag list ships in the reference implementation
(Appendix~\ref{sec:refimpl}). The implementation marks documents below ten sentences as
unreliable; the benchmark scorer requires five, and Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail} reports how
many scored generations fall between (at most \val{len.below10.max}\% of a leg's generations in
any one mode) and that a ten-sentence floor changes \val{floor10.changed.w} of the
\val{floor10.n} landings re-scored.
Re-punctuating a fixed text can move $B_n$ across much of its bounded range, so the structural
metric must never select generations alone; any use that selects by it needs a quality gate in
the sense of \citet{arxiv2025evaluating}, which we have not built. The benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling} is descriptive and selects nothing by the metric, so the guard does
not filter the scored generations; it fires on \val{guard.human.pct}\% of the human articles and
\val{guard.legs.min}--\val{guard.legs.max}\% of a leg's generations in a given mode, and
re-scoring without the flagged text changes \val{guard.changed.w} of the \val{guard.n} landings
re-scored (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}).

The metric, the guard, and the multiscale profile the guard's wash-out check reads are pure standard library in
\path{experiments/burstiness_pp.py}, with a self-check; Appendix~\ref{sec:refimpl} reproduces the full implementation. They run without a model; of the
companions above, only surprisal would need one, one more reason it stays
outside the construct.

\section{The aim problem: how reliably instructions move the construct}
\label{sec:promptceiling}

\subsection{Corpus and generation}
\label{sec:corpus}
The corpus is $200$ English Wikinews articles~\citep{wikinews2021wikinews} (CC~BY~2.5; released with the model generations~\citep{lanzo2026dataset}), dated
2016--2021 and therefore pre-model. The model receives the headline and the paired article's
word count, so topic, register and target length are held fixed within a pair and the human side
is pre-model; the facts and quotations are the model's own (achieved lengths in
Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}). The human side of each pair is the plain article body,
regenerable from the source Wikinews wikitext by a deterministic parse
(\path{experiments/ablation/clean_wikinews.py}) that, on a held-out check of
$100$ articles, reproduces the shipped human bodies exactly. The first leg is
a frozen open-weights Qwen3.6-27B (bf16,
temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.95$, reasoning mode enabled, reasoning stripped before
scoring). Eight further models join in Section~\ref{sec:ninefam}. For each headline the model
writes a news article from a brief containing only the headline and
the paired human article's word count, so each generation targets its pair's length. We compare five
non-overlapping prompting modes: a \emph{control} brief with no stylistic instruction, an
\emph{instruction} mode that adds a metric-free persona directive to write as a professional
reporter in a natural human voice, never flat or mechanical, restated near the end of the prompt,
\emph{few-shot} exemplars of matched human news prose~\citep{brown2020language, min2022rethinking},
an outline-then-write \emph{plan} mode~\citep{wang2023plan}, and a draft-critique-rewrite
\emph{self-refine} loop~\citep{madaan2023self}. The three few-shot exemplars are representative
human news reports, matched by register and topic and centered on the median $B_n$ of the
exemplar pool (each near $B_n = -0.416$; the evaluation set's median is $\val{human.median}$). The
selection uses $B_n$ only to find the exemplars nearest that median, never the high-dispersion
end, so the few-shot mode sees typical human sentence-length dispersion. No prompt names the statistic or gives a numeric target on it. Every non-control prompt asks
for prose that is never flat or mechanical; the few-shot and self-refine prompts name rhythm,
pacing, flow and cadence, and the rewrite step says to change how the sentences move, as a user
asking for human-sounding prose would. We sample each (mode, headline) cell three times, at fixed
seeds on the open-weight legs and as independent calls on the API legs, to which no seed is
sent; we call a cell's three samples its seeds throughout. Of the
leg's $3{,}000$ cells,
$2{,}996$ completed;
we excluded the four that exceeded the reasoning budget rather than scoring their incomplete output. We average affected
headlines over their remaining seeds, so every mode retains $200$ matched pairs. The entire run is
scripted, and every reported number can be recomputed from the released artifacts.\footnote{Scoring code, prompts, the metric implementation, per-model scored evaluations (normalized protocol, plus the audited leg's raw-protocol archive) and generation metadata accompany the paper; the full repository, with the research database, is at \url{https://github.com/VittoriaLanzo/burstiness-was-measured-wrong-lp4fm}, and the human articles and the raw generations of every leg and of the paraphrase probe are released as one dataset~\citep{lanzo2026dataset}.} Appendix~\ref{sec:prompts} lists the verbatim wording of every mode and the rule that composes them.

\subsection{Normalization: stripping the wrapper before scoring}
\label{sec:normalization}
Model output does not arrive as plain prose. A generation-hygiene guard flagged markdown
scaffolding and echoed headline blocks in every model we benchmark, from \val{hyg.deepseek.md} (markdown) and \val{hyg.deepseek.title} (title lines) of control-mode generations
on one leg (DeepSeek V4 Pro) to \val{hyg.gemini.md} on another (Gemini 3.1 Pro,
both features). All of it is formatting the
plain-prose human baseline lacks. A segmenter fed raw output folds a markdown header or an echoed headline into
the next sentence, or splits it off when it ends in terminal punctuation, and those pseudo-sentences manufacture length dispersion and segmentation symbols
the article prose does not have. Every generated leg is therefore normalized before scoring:
a deterministic script (\path{experiments/ablation/normalize_generation.py}) drops the echoed
headline block and unwraps markdown syntax while preserving the article prose; the human baseline
is untouched. A word-loss audit of the full first leg finds median $0.0\%$, mean
$1.7\%$, and at most $7.4\%$ of a generation lost to normalization, that maximum being the
headline block itself.

Scaffolding sits outside the construct: $B_n$ is defined over the sentence
lengths of prose, and a markdown header or an echoed headline is not a sentence of the article.
Scoring it credits formatting as dispersion, the same class of inflation the Goodhart
guard flags (Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}), so normalization is a
corpus-level Goodhart correction, applied uniformly to every leg. Its size is measurable on the
audited leg (Qwen3.6-27B): scored raw, instruction-mode $B_n$ is
\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.eqphrase} ($\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.d}$, CI
$\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.ci}$, $m^{*}=\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.mstar}$,
$\val{qwenraw.Bn.instruction.p}$), where normalized text differs significantly
($\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.d}$, CI $\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.ci}$, $\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.p}$)
and its interval needs a margin of $\val{qwen.Bn.instruction.mstar}$ to count as equivalent; a raw
control-mode gap on memory ($\val{qwenraw.M.control.d}$, $\val{qwenraw.M.control.p}$) is not
significant on normalized text ($\val{qwen.M.control.d}$, $\val{qwen.M.control.p}$), and
segmentation entropy shows no significant control-mode difference under either scoring, which is
why we describe that leg's control gap as significant only on the dispersion dimensions; and the control-mode dispersion gap on
normalized text is $\val{qwen.Bn.control.d}$ against a raw estimate of
$\val{qwenraw.Bn.control.d}$, with per-seed rank-biserial rising from
$\val{qwenraw.cons.control.min}$--$\val{qwenraw.cons.control.max}$ to
$\val{qwen.cons.control.min}$--$\val{qwen.cons.control.max}$.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
Because the three seeds of a cell share their human counterpart, pooling them as separate pairs would
pseudo-replicate the human documents and overstate significance. We therefore average each headline's
metric vector across its seeds first and run one Wilcoxon signed-rank test per mode and dimension over
the matched pairs ($\val{npairs.min}$--$\val{npairs.max}$ per model and mode). Matched
rank-biserial is reported as effect size. Each test compares one mode against the human articles;
where the text compares two modes of the same model, it cites a direct paired contrast between
them on the same headlines (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}; contrast deltas are in the model's
own $B_n$; negative means the first mode is flatter).

Every model ran the identical twenty-cell grid (five modes by four dimensions), corrected within
model with Benjamini--Hochberg at $q=0.05$, Holm reported alongside; the correction script and each
leg's census ship with the released artifacts. Across the nine models' grids, $\val{sig.raw}$ of
the $180$ cells are significant at the per-cell $0.05$ level; $\val{sig.bh}$ survive
Benjamini--Hochberg and $\val{sig.holm}$ survive Holm. The one cell that falls under
Benjamini--Hochberg (\val{sig.lostbh.names}) is not on $B_n$, and the prose does not build on it. No correction is applied across models; Table~\ref{tab:ninefam} carries
per-cell stars with a pointer to each model's corrected census in Appendix~\ref{sec:familygrids}.

Matching claims are never stated as failures to reject. A match requires the $95\%$ CI of the median
paired delta inside a pre-specified margin; margins $0.03$, $0.05$, and $0.08$ were fixed before the
cross-model runs. We also report $m^{*}$, the smallest margin the CI would clear, the larger of the
two absolute CI bounds. CIs on the median paired delta are percentile bootstrap intervals: the
$2.5$th and $97.5$th percentiles of the medians of $2{,}000$ resamples of the matched pairs.
Significance and equivalence are separate verdicts: a delta can differ significantly from zero
and still sit inside a margin, and the text reports both. We say a prompt closes a model's gap
when its cell is both not significantly different from the human articles and equivalent at a
pre-specified margin: equivalence alone would count a significant gap as closed. For scale, the $200$ human articles have
median $B_n = \val{human.median}$ with standard deviation $\val{human.sd}$, so the three margins are
$\val{human.margin0.03.sd}$, $\val{human.margin0.05.sd}$ and $\val{human.margin0.08.sd}$ of a human
standard deviation.

\subsection{One instruction for nine models}
\label{sec:ninefam}
The design of Section~\ref{sec:corpus} runs identically on every model: the same $200$ headlines,
the same five wordings, three samples per cell, uniform normalization, the same paired scoring.
The nine models are Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma 3 27B (open weights, bf16, temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.95$);
DeepSeek V4 Pro, Grok 4.3, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6 (API-served, provider-default decoding; GLM-5.2
ignores a requested temperature and Kimi K2.6 rejects one); and Grok 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and
Claude Opus 4.8 (closed APIs; Grok 4.5 exposes neither temperature nor top-$p$, Gemini 3.1 Pro ran
at temperature $1.0$ with provider-managed thinking, and Claude Opus 4.8 exposes no temperature).
Thinking modes, effort levels and output caps are recorded in each leg's released metadata and
listed in Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}. Two of the nine models' API legs used a $32{,}000$-token cap instead of
$16{,}000$: DeepSeek V4 Pro from early in its run (Appendix~\ref{sec:caps}), after reasoning exhausted the smaller cap, and Claude Opus 4.8,
because Anthropic bills reasoning against the cap, so at maximum effort the self-refine rewrite
spent the whole budget reasoning and emitted no prose. That leg ran at effort high (Gemini 3.1
Pro ran at thinking level high) and completed all $3{,}000$ cells with no truncated or empty
output. A tenth leg, labeled DeepSeek V4 Pro$^{\dagger}$ throughout, re-ran the DeepSeek V4 Pro grid on 18 October
2026 with the same prompts and briefs against the snapshot the API alias then served; it is
reported beside the nine models in every cross-model table and in Figure~\ref{fig:aimscatter},
has its own grid (Table~\ref{tab:grid-deepseek1018}), and stays outside the nine-model counts
(Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}).
Decoding is not matched across providers, so the benchmark reads as nine separate paired
comparisons against the same human baseline, each model compared against the human articles
alone. Where a provider returns reasoning content separately
we strip it before scoring. Gemini's thoughts are never returned, and Gemma 3 has no thinking mode.
We benchmark single generators only; multi-agent systems are excluded because they break the
one-generator pairing.

Completeness is disclosed per leg. Qwen3.6-27B excludes four cells for exceeded reasoning budgets
(Section~\ref{sec:corpus}), and Grok 4.5 reports two empty self-refine outputs, truncations by
signature, seed-averaged away. Provider content moderation removes cells on two legs, by two
different mechanisms. Moonshot refuses explicitly: $25$ of Kimi K2.6's $3{,}000$ cells return

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Recommendations for evaluation practice.}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{4pt}
\item Normalize before scoring: any segmenter-based feature of raw model output measures formatting as well as sentence-length dispersion, and on the leg scored both ways normalizing reverses an equivalence verdict (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).
\item Sample multiple seeds and average within headline before testing: pooling seeds pseudo-replicates the human baseline and overstates significance (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\item Audit empty cells: silent provider moderation returns ordinary responses with empty output, and unlogged empties change which headlines are compared (Section~\ref{sec:ninefam}).
\item State parity as CI-bounded equivalence against pre-specified margins and report the smallest margin cleared; a failure to reject is not a match (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Sentence-length dispersion as a detection signal.}
Sentence-length dispersion is the everyday reading of burstiness, and the benchmark says where its flatness signature holds and where it inverts. For a dispersion-based detector, the instructed flatness signature persists in \val{inst.flat.w} of nine models, but \val{anypast.names} each write significantly more dispersed text than the human articles under at least one prompted mode, where a flatness threshold would miss them, and any segmenter-based feature computed on raw output inherits the formatting artifacts of Section~\ref{sec:normalization}. The strong detector signal, surprisal, stays outside the construct.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Limits of the metric.}
Statistical separation is not construct validity: significant paired
differences show the coefficient shifts with authorship in matched news prose, and they leave open
whether it captures everything meant by rhythm.
The coefficient $B_n$ inherits the weaknesses of the coefficient of variation it is built on
(Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}): it is order-blind and shape-blind,
scoring the dispersion of the length sequence while ignoring where the long and short sentences fall, and memory recovers only
lag-1 order. At a fixed spread, sample $B_n$ still rises with sentence count, so generations with fewer sentences than their article score lower, those with more, higher (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}). The reference implementation flags documents under ten sentences; the benchmark scores from
five, and Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail} reports the sensitivity. The metric is genre-relative:
target bands do not transfer unchanged across registers. Guard thresholds are heuristic: they set a floor and prove nothing about
strategies outside their list.

\paragraph{Limits of the experiment.}
The corpus is a single register (Wikinews, expository news, English), chosen as a
conservative, low-dispersion register, so reported gaps are likely smaller than on narrative
or literary text. The nine models occupy a narrow band of contemporary instruction-tuned
assistants, with the Grok pair contributing two successive generations; for budget reasons we
omitted an OpenAI leg. The flatness tell is model- and snapshot-relative: Grok 4.3 to Grok 4.5 changes the
instruction-mode landing in a single generation, and the DeepSeek V4 Pro alias re-queried three
months later writes significantly more dispersed text than it did in July in all five modes
(Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}), so the gaps we measure should not be read as constants of
nature. One fixed wording per mode risks measuring one model's reading of one
prompt, and neither an explicit instruction to vary sentence length nor a per-model calibration
was tested, so the results show how the prompts tested move the gap, not that no prompt can close it. Replication across nine
models addresses the one-model part of that risk: few-shot output is significantly flatter than
instruction output in \val{con.fslt.phrase}. A paraphrase probe samples the one-prompt part for the instruction on one
model, the 18 October DeepSeek snapshot, where the canonical wording lands
$\val{deepseek1018.Bn.instruction.ad}$ past the human level
($\val{deepseek1018.Bn.instruction.p}$): three paraphrases of it (one sample
per brief, $\val{para.n.min}$ briefs each) land that snapshot $\val{para.ad.lo}$ to
$\val{para.ad.hi}$ past, \val{para.past.w} of three significantly, so no wording we tried returns it
to the human level and none, the canonical one included, is equivalent at $0.03$ or $0.05$.
Paired on the same headlines, the paraphrases sit $\val{para.con.d.min}$ to $\val{para.con.d.max}$
beyond the canonical wording (\val{para.con.sig.w} of three significant). The July snapshot's $0.05$ equivalence could not be re-probed:
by October the alias served a later snapshot (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}).

Decoding is not matched across providers (of the nine models' legs, four do not honor a temperature setting,
two ran at provider defaults, one closed leg ran at temperature $1.0$), so cross-model
magnitude comparisons carry a decoding confound while within-model comparisons do not,
since every mode of a leg ran under the same decoding.

The human articles may appear in the generators' training data: the 2016--2021 corpus predates all nine training cutoffs; we measured overlap directly.
An $n$-gram probe over $600$ control-mode generations per model on Qwen3.6-27B, DeepSeek V4 Pro
and Grok 4.3 finds mean matched 8-gram overlap with the paired article below $0.003$ and 13-gram
overlap below $0.001$, against a mismatched-article baseline of $0.0$ (per-model values in
Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}). Verbatim reproduction therefore cannot explain the measured gaps on the
probed models. Memorization would move dispersion toward the human level, so any unprobed
effect, whether a deficit or an overshoot, is, if contaminated, an underestimate. The direction reverses for the equivalence landings:
memorization would pull instructed dispersion toward the human level, so contamination could manufacture the equivalences. All of them fall in the instruction
mode the probe did not cover, and the probe did not cover Kimi K2.6 or Grok 4.5 at all.

\section{Conclusion}
Burstiness is measured everywhere under two readings, and the sentence-length one has lacked a
scale-free burstiness coefficient with its full range at any sentence count. This paper gives it one,
reproducible and model-free. Its first use is the aim
problem: on uniformly normalized text, \val{ctrl.flat.w} of nine models write significantly
flatter than the human articles uninstructed. The same instruction leaves \val{inst.flat.w}
significantly flatter, \val{inst.ns.w} not significantly different and \val{inst.past.w}
significantly more dispersed, and makes \val{con.widen.phrase} significantly flatter than its own
bare brief.
The gap is one that the prompts tested move but do not reliably close.

\section{Prompt wordings}
\label{sec:prompts}

The five prompting modes share one length-matched task brief and, apart from the control mode,
one persona frame. This appendix gives the exact wording of every component, the rule that
composes them into each mode, and two rules the wording leaves implicit: how the target length is
set and how the few-shot exemplars are chosen. The byte-exact source is \path{bench/prompts.py}
in the released code, which also carries a self-check that enforces the three-call structure of
the self-refine mode and the metric-free property stated below. In the blocks that follow, lines
beginning with \texttt{\#} are annotations; every other line is the literal prompt string, with
the long single-line strings wrapped to fit the page and blank lines marking the paragraph breaks
inside a string. Braced tokens such as \texttt{\{headline\}} are filled per generation.

\begin{verbatim}
# BRIEF: control mode sends only this
Write a news article with the following headline:

{headline}

Aim for about {target_words} words.

# VOICE: prepended to every non-control mode
Act as a professional news journalist. Provide the article a seasoned
staff reporter would write: concrete and specific, in a natural human
voice, never flat or mechanical.

# TAIL: appended to every non-control mode
Keep that natural, human quality all the way through, including the
closing paragraphs.

# PLAN: plan-mode body, between VOICE and BRIEF
Before you write, plan the article the way a reporter does: pick the
lede (the single most important fact), then lay out the who, what,
when, where, why, and how, and the order to present them, most
important first. Work that outline out for yourself, then write the
article to it. Do not show your planning; return only the finished
article.

# FEWSHOT_LEAD / FEWSHOT_TAIL: wrap the exemplar block
Study the voice, pacing, and rhythm of the examples below, then write
in the same spirit. Borrow only their style, never their facts or
wording.

<examples>
<example>
# exemplar 1 (full article text omitted; see "Few-shot exemplars" below)
</example>
# ... two more <example> blocks, three exemplars in total ...

</examples>

# FEEDBACK: self-refine call 2, over call 1's {draft}
You are a demanding editor. Critique the draft below only on how
naturally human its prose reads: its rhythm, pacing, and flow. Name
at least THREE specific passages that read as flat, monotonous, or
mechanical. For each, quote the exact sentence(s) and say concretely
what to change to give it a more human cadence. Do not rewrite the
draft; give only the critique. Even if the writing is already strong,
still name the three weakest spots.

DRAFT:
{draft}

# REWRITE: self-refine call 3, over {draft} and {feedback}
Here is your draft and an editor's critique.

DRAFT:
{draft}

CRITIQUE:
{feedback}

Rewrite the article in full, applying every point of the critique. This
is a substantial rewrite, not a light edit: change how the sentences
move wherever the critique flagged flatness, while preserving the
meaning, the facts, and the target length. Return only the rewritten
article.
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{How the modes compose.} The control mode sends the bare brief and nothing else. Every
other mode follows one template: the voice preamble, then a mode-specific body, then the same
brief, then the tail. The plain instruction has an empty body, so it reduces to the voice
preamble, the brief, and the tail. The plan mode puts the plan text in the body slot, and the
few-shot mode puts the exemplar block there. Self-refine is three model calls on a single
article: a draft from the instruction prompt, a critique from the feedback prompt applied to that
draft, and a rewrite from the rewrite prompt applied to the draft and the critique. A self-check
in \path{bench/prompts.py} asserts the three calls.

\paragraph{Length brief.} The \texttt{\{target\_words\}} slot is the paired human article's word
count rounded to the nearest $25$, so every generation targets its human counterpart's length
(Section~\ref{sec:corpus}).

\paragraph{Few-shot exemplars.} The exemplar block holds three straight news reports drawn from
Wikinews articles outside the $200$ evaluation set. They are the reports whose burstiness sits
nearest the median of the human distribution, chosen for register and topic. The selection uses
$B_n$ only to locate that median and drop degenerate tails, never to pick high-dispersion
exemplars, so the few-shot condition sees typical human sentence-length dispersion and no exemplar is
chosen to teach to the test. Their full texts are
committed with the released code and are omitted here for length.

\paragraph{Metric-free wording.} No prompt names the statistic the benchmark scores or gives a
numeric target on it: a self-check enforces that no prompt string contains a measured-axis
term, among them \emph{burstiness}, \emph{sentence length}, \emph{variance} and \emph{standard
deviation}, and the word count in the length brief is a length target only. Every non-control
prompt asks for prose that is never flat or mechanical; the few-shot and self-refine prompts name
rhythm, pacing, flow and cadence, and the rewrite step says to change how the sentences move, as
a user asking for human-sounding prose would. What the wording withholds is the measured
quantity and any target on it, not the intent.

\paragraph{Instruction paraphrases (wording-robustness probe).} The probe in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} re-runs the instruction mode on the 18 October DeepSeek V4 Pro
snapshot (DeepSeek V4 Pro$^{\dagger}$ in the tables) under three independent rewrites of the voice and tail above, one
sample over all $200$ briefs, each keeping the persona frame and naming no statistic. The harness is
\path{experiments/ablation/prompt_paraphrase.py}. The three pairs follow, the voice prepended and
the tail appended around the same brief.

\begin{verbatim}
# PARAPHRASE v1 (VOICE then TAIL)
You are a veteran newspaper reporter. Write the story the way
an experienced newsroom professional would: specific, grounded
in facts, with a voice that reads as unmistakably human rather
than machine-written.

Hold that authentic reporter's voice through to the final sentence.

# PARAPHRASE v2 (VOICE then TAIL)
Take the role of a seasoned journalist at a major daily. Produce
the kind of article an editor would run with light touches only:
concrete details, a living, natural voice, nothing stiff or
formulaic.

Make sure the writing still sounds fully human in the closing
paragraphs, not just the opening.

# PARAPHRASE v3 (VOICE then TAIL)
Write as a long-time staff correspondent. The article should
carry the texture of real human reportage: precise facts,
natural phrasing, and none of the flat, mechanical quality of
generated text.

Sustain that human texture to the very end of the piece.
\end{verbatim}

complete, self-contained implementation used in this paper: the sentence segmenter, the
burstiness coefficient $B_n$ (Goh--Barab\'asi under the Kim--Jo finite-$n$ correction), the
memory coefficient $M$, the Gini robustness check, Miller--Madow segmentation entropy, the
multiscale profile, and the Goodhart guard, whose thresholds are its default keyword arguments.
It runs on any Python~3 interpreter with no third-party dependencies and no reference model.
Three conventions the prose leaves to the listing: $\sigma$ in $r=\sigma/\mu$ is the population
standard deviation, the Gini divides by $n(n-1)$, and segmentation entropy is in bits over the
marks \texttt{. ! ? , ; : - ( ) " \textquotesingle} and the em dash, after typographic quotes are mapped to ASCII.

"""Reference implementation of the burstiness measurement instrument.

Model-free and pure standard library. Segments a document into sentence
lengths and computes the burstiness coefficient B_n (Goh-Barabasi under the
Kim-Jo finite-n correction), the memory coefficient M, the Gini robustness
check, Miller-Madow segmentation entropy, and a multiscale profile. The
Goodhart guard screens degenerate text that would game a dispersion score.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import re
from statistics import fmean, pstdev
from typing import List, Optional, Sequence

# Below this many sentences, a single document's B_n and M estimates are
# noisy; the entry point reports ``reliable = False``.
RELIABILITY_FLOOR = 10

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Segmentation: sentence lengths in whitespace tokens (stdlib only)
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

_ABBREVIATIONS = {
    "mr", "mrs", "ms", "dr", "prof", "sr", "jr", "st", "vs", "etc",
    "e.g", "i.e", "fig", "no", "vol", "cf", "al", "inc", "ltd", "co",
}
_SENT_SPLIT = re.compile(r'([.!?]+["\')\]]?)\s+')
_PUNCT_SYMBOLS = re.compile(r'[.!?,;:\-\u2014()"\']')
_TYPO_QUOTES = str.maketrans({"\u201c": '"', "\u201d": '"', "\u2018": "'", "\u2019": "'"})


def sentence_lengths(text: str) -> List[int]:
    """Word-token count of each sentence, with an abbreviation-guarded split."""
    if not text or not text.strip():
        return []
    text = text.translate(_TYPO_QUOTES)
    sentences: List[str] = []
    last = 0
    for m in _SENT_SPLIT.finditer(text):
        end = m.end()
        candidate = text[last:end].strip()
        bare = candidate.rstrip('.!?"\')]').strip()
        last_word = bare.split()[-1].lower() if bare.split() else ""
        if last_word in _ABBREVIATIONS:
            continue
        if candidate:
            sentences.append(candidate)
        last = end
    tail = text[last:].strip()
    if tail:
        sentences.append(tail)
    return [len(s.split()) for s in sentences if s.split()]


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Core coefficients (operate on a sequence of sentence lengths)
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def burstiness_B(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Goh-Barabasi coefficient B = (sigma - mu) / (sigma + mu), bounded [-1, 1]."""
    if len(intervals) < 2:
        return None
    mu = fmean(intervals)
    sigma = pstdev(intervals)
    denom = sigma + mu
    if denom <= 0:
        return None
    return (sigma - mu) / denom


def burstiness_B_corrected(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Kim-Jo finite-n correction B_n; reduces to (r-1)/(r+1) as n -> inf."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 2:
        return None
    mu = fmean(intervals)
    if mu <= 0:
        return None
    r = pstdev(intervals) / mu
    sp, sm = math.sqrt(n + 1), math.sqrt(n - 1)
    denom = (sp - 2.0) * r + sm
    if denom == 0:
        return None
    return (sp * r - sm) / denom


def memory_M(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Lag-1 autocorrelation of consecutive sentence lengths (the memory term)."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 3:
        return None
    a = list(intervals[:-1])
    b = list(intervals[1:])
    m1, m2 = fmean(a), fmean(b)
    s1, s2 = pstdev(a), pstdev(b)
    if s1 <= 0 or s2 <= 0:
        return None
    cov = fmean((x - m1) * (y - m2) for x, y in zip(a, b))
    return cov / (s1 * s2)


def memory_M_corrected(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """memory_M plus 1/(n-1), n the number of intervals: the M the benchmark scores."""
    m = memory_M(intervals)
    return None if m is None else m + 1.0 / (len(intervals) - 1)


def gini(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """Gini coefficient of sentence lengths; scale-free robustness check for B_n."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 2:
        return 0.0
    m = fmean(intervals)
    if m == 0:
        return 0.0
    # Sample (unbiased) Gini: divide by n*(n-1), not n^2, which removes an
    # n-dependent bias that would confound cross-document comparison.
    return sum(abs(a - b) for a in intervals for b in intervals) / (2.0 * n * (n - 1) * m)


def segmentation_entropy(text: str) -> float:
    """Miller-Madow-corrected Shannon entropy (bits) of the punctuation marks.

    Typographic quotes are normalized to ASCII first, so quote STYLE (curly vs
    straight) is not scored as segmentation diversity.
    """
    counts: dict = {}
    for ch in _PUNCT_SYMBOLS.findall((text or "").translate(_TYPO_QUOTES)):
        counts[ch] = counts.get(ch, 0) + 1
    total = sum(counts.values())
    if total == 0:
        return 0.0
    h = -sum((c / total) * math.log2(c / total) for c in counts.values())
    return h + (len(counts) - 1) / (2.0 * total * math.log(2))


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Multiscale temporal profile
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def _coarse_grain(L: Sequence[float], w: int) -> List[float]:
    """Non-overlapping window means: coarsen the length series to scale w."""
    return [fmean(L[i:i + w]) for i in range(0, len(L) - w + 1, w)]


def multiscale_burstiness(text: str, scales: Sequence[int] = (1, 2, 4)) -> dict:
    """B_n at the sentence scale and at coarse-grained window scales.

    Genuine long-range burstiness holds its dispersion at coarse scales, whereas
    mechanical alternation washes out toward B_n = -1 at scale >= 2.
    """
    L = [float(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    out = {}
    for s in scales:
        cg = list(L) if s == 1 else _coarse_grain(L, s)
        out[f"B_n@{s}"] = burstiness_B_corrected(cg) if len(cg) >= 2 else None
    return out


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Goodhart guard: flag degenerate texts that reach the score by gaming it
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def _trigram_diversity(text: str) -> float:
    """Distinct word-trigram ratio (catches verbatim repetition)."""
    w = (text or "").split()
    if len(w) < 4:
        return 1.0
    tris = list(zip(w, w[1:], w[2:]))
    return len(set(tris)) / len(tris)


def _runon_ratio(text: str) -> float:
    """Internal clause delimiters (, ; :) per sentence terminal (. ! ?)."""
    internal = len(re.findall(r"[,;:]", text or ""))
    terminal = len(re.findall(r"[.!?]+", text or "")) or 1
    return internal / terminal


def gaming_flags(text, *, loo_delta=0.25, m_floor=-0.5, m_ceiling=0.7,
                 distinct_len_min=0.5, trigram_min=0.5, frag_frac_max=0.3,
                 punct_rate_max=0.25, runon_max=8.0, finescale_drop=0.4):
    """Flag texts that reach a high dispersion score through degenerate patterns.

    Returns {"ok", "gate_ok", "flags", "detail"}. ``ok`` means no gaming pattern
    fired; ``gate_ok`` means ok AND reliable (n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR). A use that
    selects text by B_n should require gate_ok and a separate quality gate:
    re-punctuating a fixed text can swing B_n across much of its range, so B_n
    must never select generations on its own.

    Flags: outlier_driven (dropping the longest sentence swings B_n > loo_delta),
    low_len_diversity (distinct-length ratio < distinct_len_min at n >= 8, which
    catches blocky and period-k patterns), repetitive (trigram ratio <
    trigram_min), alternating (M < m_floor), over_clumped (M > m_ceiling),
    fine_scale_only (B_n@1 > 0 but drops by > finescale_drop at scale 2),
    fragment_spam (>= frag_frac_max of sentences under 3 words), punct_gamed
    (punctuation marks per word > punct_rate_max), runon (internal-to-terminal
    delimiter ratio > runon_max).
    """
    Li = [int(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    L = [float(x) for x in Li]
    n = len(L)
    if n < 3:
        return {"ok": False, "gate_ok": False, "flags": {"too_short": True}, "detail": {"n": n}}

    B_full = burstiness_B_corrected(L)
    j = max(range(n), key=lambda i: L[i])
    B_loo = burstiness_B_corrected(L[:j] + L[j + 1:])
    loo = abs(B_full - B_loo) if (B_full is not None and B_loo is not None) else 0.0
    M = memory_M(L)  # the guard's thresholds are set on the uncorrected coefficient
    distinct_ratio = len(set(Li)) / n
    trig = _trigram_diversity(text)
    frag_frac = sum(1 for x in Li if x < 3) / n
    words = sum(L) or 1.0
    punct_rate = len(_PUNCT_SYMBOLS.findall(text or "")) / words
    runon = _runon_ratio(text)
    ms = multiscale_burstiness(text)
    b1, b2 = ms.get("B_n@1"), ms.get("B_n@2")
    fine_scale_only = (b1 is not None and b2 is not None and b1 > 0.0
                       and (b1 - b2) > finescale_drop)

    flags = {
        "outlier_driven": loo > loo_delta,
        "low_len_diversity": n >= 8 and distinct_ratio < distinct_len_min,
        "repetitive": trig < trigram_min,
        "alternating": M is not None and M < m_floor,
        "over_clumped": M is not None and M > m_ceiling,
        "fine_scale_only": fine_scale_only,
        "fragment_spam": frag_frac >= frag_frac_max,
        "punct_gamed": punct_rate > punct_rate_max,
        "runon": runon > runon_max,
    }
    ok = not any(flags.values())
    return {
        "ok": ok,
        "gate_ok": ok and n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR,
        "flags": flags,
        "detail": {"n": n, "loo_dB": round(loo, 3),
                   "M": None if M is None else round(M, 3),
                   "distinct_ratio": round(distinct_ratio, 3), "trigram_div": round(trig, 3),
                   "frag_frac": round(frag_frac, 3), "punct_rate": round(punct_rate, 3),
                   "runon": round(runon, 2)},
    }


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point: the full model-free vector for a document
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def burstiness_pp(text: str) -> dict:
    """Model-free structural burstiness of a document.

    B_n is the primary dispersion coefficient, M the lag-1 memory, Gini the
    robustness check, and multiscale the temporal profile. Segmentation entropy
    is a separate axis (see segmentation_entropy) and surprisal is kept outside
    the construct. ``reliable`` is False below RELIABILITY_FLOOR sentences.
    """
    L = [float(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    n = len(L)
    return {
        "B_n": burstiness_B_corrected(L),
        "M": memory_M_corrected(L),
        "gini": gini(L),
        "multiscale": multiscale_burstiness(text),
        "n_sentences": n,
        "reliable": n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR,
        "sentence_lengths": [int(x) for x in L],
    }
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Protocol and audit detail}\label{sec:protocoldetail}

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 294--299
\subsection{Corpus parse mechanics}
The human side of each pair is the plain article body, regenerable from the source Wikinews
wikitext by a deterministic MediaWiki parse (\path{experiments/ablation/clean_wikinews.py})
that keeps the body up to the first section heading or reader-comment block, removes templates,
drops reference tags with their contents, resolves wikilinks to their display text, and unwraps
inline formatting. On a held-out check of $100$ articles this parse reproduces the shipped
human bodies exactly.

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 315--319 and 402--412
\subsection{Per-leg completeness}
Completeness is disclosed per leg. Of Qwen3.6-27B's $3{,}000$ cells ($200$ headlines $\times$
$5$ modes $\times$ $3$ seeds), $2{,}996$ completed; we excluded the four that exceeded the
reasoning budget rather than scoring their incomplete output. We average affected headlines
over their remaining seeds, so every mode retains $200$ matched pairs. Grok 4.5 reports two
empty self-refine outputs, truncations by signature, seed-averaged away. Provider content
moderation removes cells on two legs, by two different mechanisms. Moonshot's filter refuses
explicitly: $25$ of Kimi K2.6's $3{,}000$ cells return HTTP $400$, one Hong Kong protest
headline refused at every mode and seed. Zhipu's filter refuses silently: $36$ of GLM-5.2's
cells return an ordinary HTTP $200$ with empty output, concentrated on the same cluster of
politically sensitive headlines, leaving \val{glm.npairs.range} paired headlines per mode on the
GLM-5.2 leg and \val{kimi.npairs.range} on the Kimi K2.6 leg (per-mode counts in the grid
captions of Appendix~\ref{sec:familygrids}). The remaining five legs generated all $200$
headlines in every mode.

\subsection{Per-leg decoding and output caps}\label{sec:caps}
GLM-5.2 ignores a requested temperature and Kimi K2.6 rejects one; Grok 4.5 exposes neither
temperature nor top-$p$; Gemini 3.1 Pro ran at temperature $1.0$ with provider-managed thinking;
Claude Opus 4.8 exposes no temperature. Gemini's thoughts are never returned, and Gemma 3 has
no thinking mode. Output caps: $16{,}000$ tokens on the API legs, except DeepSeek V4 Pro, whose
cap was raised from $16{,}000$ to $32{,}000$ after $51$ of its first $513$ cells spent the whole
budget on reasoning (those cells were regenerated at the larger cap and the other early cells
kept), and Claude Opus 4.8 at $32{,}000$; on the open-weight legs the cap is per article, $6{,}144$ tokens
on Qwen3.6-27B, whose thinking shares the budget, and $1{,}024$ on Gemma 3 27B. Anthropic bills
reasoning against the cap, so at $16{,}000$ tokens and maximum effort the Opus self-refine rewrite
spent the whole budget reasoning and emitted no prose; the leg ran at high effort with the
$32{,}000$-token cap, which restores a comparable prose budget rather than granting a larger one;
Gemini 3.1 Pro ran at thinking level high. The Opus leg completed all $3{,}000$ cells with no
truncated or empty output. Each cell was sampled three times: at seeds $0$, $1$, $2$ on the open-weight
legs, as independent calls on the API legs.

\subsection{Confidence intervals}
CIs on median paired deltas are percentile bootstrap intervals: the $2.5$th and $97.5$th
percentiles of the medians of $2{,}000$ resamples of the matched pairs. The matched rank-biserial
is $(W^{+}-W^{-})/(W^{+}+W^{-})$ over the non-zero paired deltas, and a sample on which a metric
is undefined is dropped from that headline's seed mean. Per-seed $B_n$
effect sizes are released alongside the evaluation artifacts and are consistent with the
seed-averaged results.

\subsection{Segmentation audit}
The sentence segmenter splits on terminal punctuation followed by a space, optionally with an
ASCII closing quote or bracket in between. The segmenter as first released did not list typographic closing
quotes, so a sentence ending in \texttt{.}\textquotedblright{} followed by a space was not split from the next one.
The human articles carry straight quotes and no such boundaries; several API models emit
typographic quotes, with a leg mean of up to $1.3$ missed boundaries per generation on the DeepSeek V4 Pro,
Gemma 3 27B and Grok 4.5 legs, merges that on net make the model look more dispersed. The
current version maps typographic quotes to ASCII before segmentation, and every benchmark number in this
paper is scored with the corrected segmenter; the correction moved median $\Delta B_n$ by
$\val{segfix.shift.min}$ to $\val{segfix.shift.max}$ and changed \val{segfix.moved.w} of the
forty-five landings (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). Abbreviations outside the guard list, \emph{U.S.}
among them, cause a false split at rates of the same order on both sides ($0.17$ per human
article, $0.07$ to $0.26$ per generation) and are left as they are.

\subsection{Contamination probe values}
An $n$-gram contamination probe over $600$ control-mode generations per model on three models
finds mean matched 8-gram overlap with the paired article of $0.00055$ (Qwen3.6-27B, max
$0.036$), $0.00207$ (DeepSeek V4 Pro, max $0.104$), and $0.00098$ (Grok 4.3, max $0.062$),
against a mismatched-article baseline mean of $0.0$ everywhere; 13-gram means are at or below
$0.00064$ for all three. The worst single generation shares roughly $10\%$ of its 8-grams with
its paired article, consistent with a shared quotation rather than memorization. Verbatim
reproduction therefore cannot explain the measured gaps on the three probed models, which
span open-weights and API-served models. The probe covered three of the nine models, on the
control mode only.
