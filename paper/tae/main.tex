\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Burstiness is a signal people already use to judge machine text, and its
operational definition in practice is an unreviewed detector heuristic
confounded with length: raw sentence-length variance grows with the mean.
We treat sentence boundaries as a point process in word-time, scored with
the Goh--Barab\'asi coefficient under the Kim--Jo finite-$n$ correction:
bounded and scale-free, no model in the loop, screened by a Goodhart guard
and a coherence gate. The protocol is paired evaluation on $200$ era-,
topic-, and register-matched Wikinews articles dated 2016--2021: five prompting
modes, nine families, three seeds, uniform normalization before scoring. Scored raw, markdown scaffolding
manufactures dispersion and produces a parity verdict that normalization
reverses into a significant $0.028$ deficit ($p=3\times10^{-5}$); the
uninstructed dispersion gap more than doubles ($0.043$ to $0.097$). Of the forty-five family-by-mode
cells, thirty-two land significantly flatter than the human articles,
eight show no significant difference, and five land significantly more
dispersed. The same instruction leaves four families short, lands two on
the human level (CI-bounded equivalence), moves one further away, and
pushes two past it, and few-shot exemplars of typical human rhythm are
the flattest mode in seven of nine families.
We model human rhythm; we do not build detector evasion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Burstiness, sentence-length variation that gives human prose its rhythm,
is one of the most cited signals separating human writing from machine
writing~\citep{arxiv2024detecting, arxiv2025detecting}. The deployed
operational definition traces to an unreviewed detector technical
note~\citep{arxiv2024detecting, arxiv2025training}, and its statistic,
raw sentence-length variance, is confounded with absolute length:
uniformly long sentences can score burstier than genuinely varied short
ones. This is a measurement-validity failure in an evaluation signal
already deployed at scale. The paper's first subject is the evaluation
signal; the models come second. Sentence-length dispersion has been a
stylometric variable since \citet{yule1939sentence, williams1940note},
never given a reproducible measure that separates rhythm from length.
We propose one and use it to benchmark the prompting techniques people
already rely on.

\paragraph{A measurement.}
We treat sentence boundaries as a point process in word-time and score
the sentence-length sequence with the Goh--Barab\'asi burstiness
coefficient~\citep{goh2008burstiness} under the Kim--Jo finite-$n$
correction~\citep{kim2016measuring}, written $B_n$. It is bounded in
$[-1, 1]$, needs no reference model, and, as a function of the
coefficient of variation alone, measures rhythm relative to typical
sentence length instead of conflating the two.
Alongside $B_n$ we report a Gini robustness
check~\citep{gini1912variabilit}, Miller--Madow-corrected punctuation
entropy as a separate segmentation axis, and a memory coefficient $M$
for lag-1 order; surprisal stays deliberately outside the construct.
A metric that becomes a target invites gaming, so we pair $B_n$
with two safeguards, a Goodhart guard and a coherence
gate~\citep{arxiv2025evaluating} (Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}).

\paragraph{A benchmark of five prompting techniques.}
We evaluate five prompting modes people already rely on, from a bare
control brief through instruction, few-shot exemplars, outline-then-write
planning, and a self-refine loop (Section~\ref{sec:corpus}), against
$200$ English Wikinews articles dated 2016--2021~\citep{wikinews2021wikinews},
each generation length-matched to its paired article and sampled at
three seeds per cell; era, topic, and register are matched. Generated text is uniformly normalized before
scoring because markdown scaffolding and echoed headlines manufacture
dispersion the human baseline lacks (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}). Nine families span
open-weight checkpoints, API-served models, and closed frontier systems
(Section~\ref{sec:ninefam}).

\paragraph{The aim problem.}
Prompting changes rhythm but cannot steer it reliably. Uninstructed,
eight of nine families write significantly flatter than the human
articles; only Grok 4.5 starts at the human level. The identical
instruction scatters the families short of, onto, and past the human
level, a family's uninstructed gap does not predict where it lands, and
few-shot exemplars of typical human rhythm are the flattest mode in
seven of nine.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A reproducible, model-free, scale-free burstiness measure for
evaluation or control: Goh--Barab\'asi coefficient
under Kim--Jo finite-$n$ correction, companions
(Gini check, segmentation-diversity axis, memory coefficient), surprisal
kept outside, and two anti-gaming safeguards
(Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}).
\item An evaluation protocol whose integrity machinery is the
contribution: paired era-, topic-, and register-matched design;
uniform normalization whose effect on verdicts is measured;
seed-averaging avoiding pseudo-replication; within-family multiplicity
correction; parity as CI-bounded equivalence with pre-specified margins;
contamination probe; moderation-refusal audit
(Sections~\ref{sec:corpus}--\ref{sec:analysis}).
\item The aim problem, nine families, five modes, three seeds:
identical instruction scatters families short of, onto, and past the
human level, and few-shot is the flattest mode in seven of nine
(Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Burstiness research splits into two camps that rarely cite each other: measurement and detection, and steering frozen models toward style targets. Neither camp controls rhythm in-generation, and neither gives burstiness a reproducible definition. That gap is where this paper sits.

\subsection{Burstiness in detection}
The operational definition of burstiness in current practice originates in a detector technical note (Tian/GPTZero), which pairs perplexity with a single burstiness scalar~\citep{arxiv2024detecting, arxiv2025training}. It is widely used and not peer-reviewed; Section~\ref{sec:formalizing} closes exactly this trust gap. \citet{onan2025you} compare diffusion and autoregressive text stylometrically with burstiness among the features and quantify after the fact without steering it. DivEye~\citep{arxiv2025diveye} tracks how unpredictability fluctuates across a text, the nearest text-side analog to timing variation, a post-hoc score. Surveys and method papers~\citep{arxiv2025detecting} treat perplexity-burstiness as a standard pair and document its vulnerability to paraphrasing.

\subsection{Stylistic variation, diversity, and steering machinery}
The stylistic-variation benchmark~\citep{arxiv2025benchmark} evaluates frontier models across prompt settings and benchmarks variation with no distributional target to control against. Effective semantic diversity~\citep{arxiv2025evaluating} gates diversity on quality, and we adopt that method as the composite's coherence gate so incoherent high-variance text is not rewarded when the metric serves as a target.

Steering machinery exists for style axes other than rhythm: activation-level style vectors, contrastive personal style vectors, steering surveys and LoRA catalogs, and fingerprint-LoRA generation~\citep{konen2024style, liu2025personalized, turner2026weights, arxiv2025low, arxiv2026plug}, with interpolation, obfuscation, personas, and reward-guided decoding~\citep{arxiv2024continuous, fisher2024styleremix, arxiv2026styles, arxiv2026card, arxiv2025llmbraces}; all steer something, none steer rhythm. Mirostat~\citep{basu2021mirostat} is the closest mechanism precedent: controlling a distributional statistic in-generation through closed-form feedback, a token-level first moment, whereas burstiness is a second moment of a derived sequence at the sentence-boundary decision. Detector-evasion humanizers such as SICO~\citep{lu2024large} force length variation by prompt-level substitution, post-hoc and black-box, which we are not building. Speech synthesis met the same average-prosody problem and solved it with explicit prosodic conditioning during generation~\citep{raitio2020controllable, raitio2022emphasis}; listeners stop identifying a voice clone with its source when expressive prosody is mismatched~\citep{bakkouche2025prosodic}, and brain-side long-range prediction~\citep{caucheteux2023long} is motivation only.

\section{Formalizing burstiness}\label{sec:formalizing}

We replace the field's unreviewed operational definition (Section~\ref{sec:related}) with a
model-free, reproducible measurement instrument. The question, asked directly of burstiness,
is what construct the protocol measures.

\subsection{Burstiness as a point process}
Let a document $D$ be segmented into sentences with word-length sequence $L = (l_1, \dots, l_n)$. We
treat the sentence boundaries as a point process in word-time, so $L$ is a sequence of inter-event
intervals, and score it with the Goh--Barab\'asi burstiness coefficient~\citep{goh2008burstiness},
\[
B = \frac{\sigma - \mu}{\sigma + \mu},
\]
where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of $L$. $B$ is bounded in $[-1, 1]$:
$B = -1$ for a metronomic sequence, $B = 0$ for a Poisson one, and $B \to 1$ for
a maximally bursty one. Real documents give short interval sequences, so we apply the Kim--Jo
finite-$n$ correction~\citep{kim2016measuring}, writing $B_n$ for the corrected coefficient.

$B_n$ is \emph{scale-free}: as a function of the coefficient of variation $r = \sigma / \mu$ alone,
$B = (r-1)/(r+1)$, it measures variation \emph{relative} to typical sentence length, so rhythm is not
conflated with length. Classically, $B_n$ is a bounded reparametrization of the coefficient of
variation, weak alone in the stylometric literature~\citep{yule1939sentence, williams1940note}.
The novelty here is the measurement protocol around the statistic: anti-gaming
screens, the paired matched-corpus design, and the nine-family audit
(Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}).

\subsection{The construct and its companions}
Burstiness proper is the scale-free coefficient $B_n$. We report four companions, none folded into
the construct:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gini robustness check.} The Gini coefficient~\citep{gini1912variabilit} of the sentence
lengths is a scale-free dispersion measure that aggregates over all pairwise differences, so it is
less hostage to a single long sentence. It agrees with $B_n$ in direction on every significant cell
of the nine-family benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}), so no reported result is an
estimator artifact.
\item \textbf{Segmentation diversity, a separate axis.} The Miller--Madow-corrected Shannon entropy of
the punctuation distribution scores how varied the segmentation symbols are. Typographic quotes are
normalized to ASCII uniformly across all nine legs, so smart-quote typography cannot register as
diversity.
\item \textbf{Memory, the order term.} The Goh--Barab\'asi memory coefficient
$M$~\citep{goh2008burstiness}, the lag-1 correlation between consecutive interval lengths, is the
order information a dispersion statistic discards.
\item \textbf{Predictability, kept outside.} Mean per-token surprisal and mean absolute consecutive
difference, under a fixed reference model, form the strong but model-relative detector signal. They
sit deliberately \emph{outside} the controllable construct; the benchmark scores the four model-free
dimensions only.
\end{itemize}
We compute raw sentence-length variance $\mathrm{var}(L)$ only as a diagnostic; it is confounded with
absolute length by construction, the central defect of the colloquial statistic.

\subsection{Anti-gaming screens and reproducibility}
A metric that becomes a control or training target invites gaming. Degenerate texts can reach a
high-dispersion score (one giant sentence among fragments, mechanical alternation, punctuation spam);
the Goodhart guard flags these; its thresholds are heuristic, a screen rather than a validated
defense (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), and the full flag list ships in the reference
implementation (Appendix~\ref{sec:refimpl}). A reliability floor of ten sentences bounds $B_n$. Re-punctuating a
fixed text can move $B_n$ across much of its bounded range. The structural metric must never select
generations alone; the composite pairs it with a coherence gate~\citep{arxiv2025evaluating}, so
incoherent high-variance text cannot win the metric. The benchmark of
Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling} is descriptive and selects nothing by the metric; the guard and the
gate enter as design constraints on future control use.
The metric, the guard, and the multiscale profile that the guard's wash-out check reads are pure standard library in
\path{experiments/burstiness_pp.py}, with a self-check; Appendix~\ref{sec:refimpl} reproduces the full implementation. They run without a model; of the
companions above, only surprisal would need one, one more reason it stays
outside the construct.

\section{The aim problem: how reliably instructions move the construct}
\label{sec:promptceiling}

Prompting is the baseline every practitioner uses. We evaluate that
evaluation practice: how much of the rhythm gap it closes, and how
predictably, with verdicts that must hold across families.

\subsection{Corpus and generation}
\label{sec:corpus}
The corpus is $200$ English Wikinews articles~\citep{wikinews2021wikinews}
(CC~BY~2.5), dated 2016--2021, before model-written news was a plausible
presence, so the human side of each pair is human-authored, with era,
topic, and register held fixed. The human side
of each pair is the plain article body, regenerable from the source Wikinews
wikitext by a deterministic parse
(\path{experiments/ablation/clean_wikinews.py}; Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}).
The first leg is
a frozen open-weights Qwen3.6-27B (bf16, temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.95$,
reasoning mode enabled, reasoning stripped before scoring). For each headline the model writes
a news article from a brief containing only the headline and the paired human
article's word count, so each generation targets its pair's length.

We compare five non-overlapping prompting modes: a \emph{control} brief with
no stylistic instruction, an \emph{instruction} mode that adds a metric-free
persona directive to write as a professional reporter in a natural human
voice, never flat or mechanical,
\emph{few-shot} exemplars of matched human news
prose~\citep{brown2020language, min2022rethinking}, an outline-then-write
\emph{plan} mode~\citep{wang2023plan}, and a draft-critique-rewrite
\emph{self-refine} loop~\citep{madaan2023self}. The three few-shot exemplars
are matched human news reports nearest the median of the human $B_n$
distribution (each near $B_n = -0.416$), typical rhythm rather than the
high-dispersion end. No prompt names the measured axis, so no mode teaches to
the test.

We sample each (mode, headline) cell at three fixed seeds. Of the leg's
$3{,}000$ cells, $2{,}996$ completed; we excluded the four that exceeded the
reasoning budget rather than scoring their incomplete output, and affected
headlines average over their remaining seeds, so every mode retains $200$
matched pairs. The entire run is scripted and reproducible from the released
artifacts.\footnote{Scoring code, prompts, the metric implementation, per-family scored evaluations (normalized protocol, plus the audited leg's raw-protocol archive), and generation metadata accompany the paper as supplementary material. The full anonymized repository, adding raw generations for eight of nine legs and the research database, is at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/burstiness-was-measured-wrong}. De-anonymized links appear in the camera-ready.}
Appendix~\ref{sec:prompts} lists the verbatim wording of every mode and the
rule that composes them.

\subsection{Normalization: stripping the wrapper before scoring}
\label{sec:normalization}
Model output does not arrive as plain prose. A generation-hygiene guard
flagged markdown scaffolding and echoed headline blocks in every family, from
23--38\% of control-mode generations on one leg to 100\% on another
(Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}), formatting the plain-prose human
articles lack. A segmenter fed raw
output scores a markdown header or an echoed dateline as a short
pseudo-sentence, manufacturing length dispersion and segmentation
symbols the article prose lacks.

Every generated leg is normalized before scoring by a deterministic script
(\path{experiments/ablation/normalize_generation.py}). The script drops the
echoed headline block and unwraps markdown syntax while preserving the
article prose; the human baseline is untouched. A word-loss audit of the full
first leg finds median $0.0\%$, mean $1.7\%$, and at most $7.4\%$ of a
generation lost to normalization; the maximum is the headline block itself. Scaffolding sits outside the construct: scoring it credits formatting
as rhythm, the same inflation class the Goodhart guard rejects
(Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}); normalization is the corpus-level
correction applied uniformly to every leg.

On the audited leg (Qwen3.6-27B), scored raw, instruction-mode $B_n$ shows
parity ($0.005$, $p=0.10$) where normalized text shows a significant deficit
($0.028$, $p=3\times10^{-5}$); raw control-mode gaps on segmentation entropy
and memory ($0.038$ and $-0.060$, both significant) vanish on normalized
text ($-0.001$ and $-0.007$); and the control-mode dispersion gap on
normalized text is more than twice the raw estimate ($0.043$ to $0.097$).
Any benchmark or detector feature computed on raw model output inherits
these artifacts, so an unreported normalization choice can flip an
evaluation's conclusion in either direction.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
Because the three seeds of a cell share their human counterpart, pooling them as separate pairs would
pseudo-replicate the human documents and overstate significance. We therefore average each headline's
metric vector across seeds first and run one Wilcoxon signed-rank test per mode and dimension over
the $200$ independent matched pairs. The matched rank-biserial is the effect size. Each test compares
one mode against the human articles. A family's narrowing from one mode to another compares two
estimates and is not itself a tested contrast.

Every family ran the identical twenty-cell grid (five modes by four dimensions). Each grid is
corrected within family with Benjamini--Hochberg at $q=0.05$; Holm is reported alongside.
Across the nine grids, $124$ of the $180$ cells are significant at the per-cell $0.05$ level;
$120$ survive Benjamini--Hochberg and $103$ survive Holm. All four cells that fall under
Benjamini--Hochberg sit on companion dimensions, none on $B_n$. The one the prose discusses,
Qwen3.6-27B instruction-mode memory ($p=0.045$), we mark exploratory and do not build on.
No correction is applied across families. Table~\ref{tab:ninefam} carries per-cell stars, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:familygrids} reports each family's corrected census.

Matching claims are never stated as failures to reject. A match requires the $95\%$ CI of the
median paired delta to fall inside a pre-specified margin; the margins $0.03$, $0.05$, and $0.08$
were fixed before the cross-family runs. We also report $m^{*}$, the smallest margin the CI would
clear, that is, the larger of the two absolute CI bounds. CIs are percentile bootstrap intervals
(Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}).

\subsection{Nine model families: one instruction, nine landings}
\label{sec:ninefam}
The design of Section~\ref{sec:corpus} runs identically on every family.
The nine families are Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma 3 27B (open weights, bf16, temperature $0.7$,
top-$p$ $0.95$); DeepSeek V4 Pro, Grok 4.3, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6 (API-served,
provider-default decoding); and Grok 4.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Claude Opus 4.8 (closed). Four
legs do not honor a requested temperature. Per-leg decoding, thinking modes, effort levels,
and output caps are recorded in the released metadata, and the one output-cap deviation, which
restores prose-budget parity for Claude Opus 4.8, is explained in
Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}.

Decoding is not matched across providers, so the benchmark reads as nine separate paired
comparisons against the same human baseline. Where a provider returns reasoning content
separately, we strip it before scoring. We benchmark single generators only; multi-agent
systems break the one-generator pairing.

Completeness is disclosed per leg. Provider content moderation removes cells on two legs by two
different mechanisms: Moonshot refuses explicitly ($25$ of Kimi K2.6's $3{,}000$ cells, one
Hong Kong protest headline refused at every mode and seed), and Zhipu refuses silently ($36$ of
GLM-5.2's cells return an ordinary response with empty output, concentrated on the same
politically sensitive cluster). The harness records both as empty cells
and seed-averaging excludes them, so every paired statistic is computed over the headlines the
model actually wrote; a benchmark that does not audit empty outputs would silently absorb the second mechanism into its scores.
Grok 4.5's two empty cells are truncations by signature
(Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}); the remaining five legs generated all $200$ headlines in
every mode.

Table~\ref{tab:ninefam} reports the $B_n$ cell for every mode on every family,
Figure~\ref{fig:aimscatter} plots all forty-five cells, and
Appendix~\ref{sec:familygrids} carries each family's full four-dimension grid.

Uninstructed, eight of nine families write significantly flatter than the human articles,
with control deficits from $0.027$ to $0.128$. Grok 4.5 alone starts at the human level
($-0.009$, $p=0.086$). The three largest gaps are the frontier flagships, Gemini 3.1 Pro
($0.128$), GLM-5.2 ($0.126$), and Claude Opus 4.8 ($0.119$), which start within $0.009$ of
one another, so frontier scale does not close the gap.
Deficits of at most $0.128$ on a coefficient bounded in $[-1,1]$ are consistent signatures,
well short of the gross flatness the detector literature describes~\citep{arxiv2024detecting, arxiv2025detecting}.

On four families (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GLM-5.2, Qwen3.6-27B, Gemma 3 27B) the instruction narrows
the gap without closing it (Table~\ref{tab:ninefam}).

DeepSeek V4 Pro and Kimi K2.6 land on the human dispersion level. DeepSeek V4 Pro closes a significant control gap
($0.035$, rank-biserial $0.34$, $p=3\times10^{-5}$) to a median delta of $0.000$ with $95\%$ CI
$[-0.012, 0.026]$ ($p=0.57$), inside the tightest margin, $m^{*}=0.026$. Kimi K2.6 closes
$0.095$ ($0.69$) to $-0.006$ with CI $[-0.034, 0.020]$ ($p=0.96$), $m^{*}=0.034$.
The DeepSeek landing is one-dimensional. Its instructed leg overshoots human segmentation
entropy ($-0.145$, rank-biserial $-0.47$, $p<10^{-5}$) while matching dispersion
(Appendix~\ref{sec:familygrids}).

Five of the forty-five cells land significantly past the human articles. Claude Opus 4.8 and
Grok 4.5 cross under instruction ($-0.037$, rank-biserial $-0.25$, $p=0.002$, surviving both
corrections; $-0.025$, $-0.20$, $p=0.015$, surviving Benjamini--Hochberg, falling under Holm).
Self-refine pushes three families past (Kimi K2.6 $-0.070$, Claude Opus 4.8 $-0.065$,
Grok 4.5 $-0.059$, all surviving both corrections). Instruction widens Grok 4.3's gap from
$0.027$ to $0.050$, and Grok 4.5, one generation later, inverts past the human level.

All nine families undershoot significantly under few-shot. The few-shot gap exceeds the
family's control gap in six of nine, and ranked by rank-biserial it is the largest-effect
mode in seven of nine (rank-biserial $0.65$ to $0.94$). The exceptions,
DeepSeek V4 Pro and Claude Opus 4.8, are the families whose uninstructed control carries
the larger effect. On Grok 4.5 few-shot alone drags the model back below the human level
($0.077$, rank-biserial $0.69$, $p<10^{-5}$). The exemplars sit at the median of the human
rhythm distribution (Section~\ref{sec:corpus}). Showing the model
typical human rhythm produces text farther from it than saying nothing, in six of nine
families; an untested reading is register reproduction rather than variance.

The synthesis is the scatter. Across the forty-five family-by-mode cells, thirty-two land
significantly flatter than the human articles, eight show no significant difference, and five
land significantly more dispersed. The same instruction, word for word, leaves four families
significantly short of the human dispersion level, lands two on it, moves one further away, and
pushes two significantly past. Prompt-level control has poor aim: its gain and even its sign are
family-dependent, and the uninstructed gap does not predict the landing. This is what the
diagnostic exposes: the gap it measures cannot be closed reliably from the prompt side, because
the instruction that reaches parity on one family moves the next one a different amount and
sometimes the wrong way.
A verdict about machine ``flatness'' estimated on one family, one mode, or one seed generalizes in no direction, which is the sense in which the evaluation, and not only the models, fails the trust test.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What this audit prescribes for evaluation practice.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Normalize before scoring: segmenter-based features computed on raw output measure formatting as well as rhythm, and the unreported choice can flip verdicts (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).
\item Sample multiple seeds and average within each headline before testing: pooling seeds pseudo-replicates the human baseline and overstates significance (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\item Audit empty cells: silent provider moderation returns ordinary responses with empty output, and unlogged empties reshape scores invisibly (Section~\ref{sec:ninefam}).
\item State parity as CI-bounded equivalence against pre-specified margins and report the smallest margin cleared; a failure to reject is not a match (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Trusting burstiness as a detection signal.}
\looseness=-1
A public metric or future controller that raises burstiness could be read as an anti-detection tool; we resist that framing. The measure is exercised in a controlled paired design, as useful for building better detectors as for producing more human-like text. For detection, the instructed dispersion signature persists in five of nine families, but Grok 4.5, Kimi K2.6, and Claude Opus 4.8 each write at or beyond human dispersion under at least one prompted mode, where a flatness threshold fires in the wrong direction. We report detector-relevant behavior rather than optimizing to defeat any one detector; the strong detector signal (surprisal) stays outside the construct.

\paragraph{Dual use and disclosure.}
We disclose AI-assisted research per venue policy and benchmark against detectors transparently. Future voice and fingerprint work proceeds only from a person's writing with their participation.

\paragraph{What the diagnostic leaves open.}
For the five modes tested, the landing cannot be aimed (Section~\ref{sec:promptceiling}), and parity is partial: no leg carries an equivalence bound on the whole metric vector. Rhythm is a sentence-boundary property; aiming may require intervention at that altitude.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Limits of the metric.}
Statistical separation is not construct validity: significant paired
differences show the coefficient separates human from model text in matched news prose, and
they leave open whether it captures everything meant by rhythm. We have not validated the
coefficient against blinded human rhythm judgments; until then it is a proposed measure.
The coefficient $B_n$ inherits the weaknesses of the coefficient of variation it reparametrizes
(Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}): it is order-blind and shape-blind, and the memory coefficient
recovers only lag-1 order. The metric is unreliable below its ten-sentence floor
(Section~\ref{sec:formalizing}).
Syntactic complexity is work in progress and remains outside the locked construct.
Goodhart guard thresholds are heuristic: a floor, proving nothing about strategies outside
their list.

\paragraph{Limits of the experiment.}
\looseness=-1
The corpus is a single register (Wikinews, expository news, English), chosen because expository
prose is conservative and low-rhythm; reported gaps are likely smaller than narrative text
would show. Pairs match era, topic, register, and length, not propositional content
(Section~\ref{sec:corpus}): a story dense in quotation and figures may pace its sentences
differently from one improvised from a headline, so part of each gap may be content
rather than authorship.
Nine families occupy a narrow band of contemporary instruction-tuned assistants; the Grok
pair shares one vendor; budget excluded an OpenAI leg. Results are not yet
shown across genres or languages. The tell keeps changing: Grok 4.3
to 4.5 flips the instruction-mode gap in one generation, so size and sign are
vintage-relative. One fixed wording per mode risks
measuring one model's reading of one prompt; cross-family replication blunts that risk.
DeepSeek's three metric-free paraphrases land instruction at or past the human level on $B_n$
(median deltas $-0.026$ to $-0.030$ against $+0.000$); the mode conclusion holds
under rewording, with a wording-sensitivity band of about $0.03$.

Decoding is not matched across providers: four legs do not honor temperature, two ran
provider defaults, one closed leg ran at $1.0$. Cross-family magnitude comparisons
carry a decoding confound; within-family comparisons do not. Equivalence margins are
analysis-time judgments; $0.03$ on a $[-1,1]$ coefficient is a claim of practical
insignificance.

\looseness=-1
The 2016--2021 corpus predates all nine training cutoffs; we measured overlap directly. An
$n$-gram probe on three families (Appendix~\ref{sec:protocoldetail}) finds matched 8-gram means
at or below $0.00207$ against a mismatched-article baseline of $0.0$; the worst single
generation is consistent with a shared quotation, not memorization. Verbatim reproduction
cannot explain the gaps on the probed families. Memorization would move dispersion toward the
human level, so any unprobed gap is, if contaminated, an underestimate. The direction reverses
for the parity landings, which fall in the instruction mode the probe did not cover:
contamination could manufacture the equivalences we report. Kimi K2.6 is unprobed.

\section{Conclusion}
\looseness=-1
Burstiness has been measured everywhere and, in detection practice, formalized in an
unreviewed detector note; this paper gives it a reproducible, model-free measure
with declared anti-gaming screens, and uses it to expose the aim problem: eight of nine
families write significantly flatter than the human articles
uninstructed, the same instruction scatters them short of, onto, and past the human level,
and few-shot is the flattest mode in seven of nine.
Whether the gap can be closed from inside the generation loop is the question the instrument
now makes answerable.

\section{Prompt wordings}
\label{sec:prompts}

The five prompting modes share one length-matched task brief and, apart from the control mode,
one persona frame. This appendix gives the exact wording of every component, the rule that
composes them into each mode, and two rules the wording leaves implicit: how the target length is
set and how the few-shot exemplars are chosen. The byte-exact source is \path{bench/prompts.py}
in the released code, which also carries a self-check that enforces the three-call structure of
the self-refine mode and the metric-free property stated below. In the blocks that follow, lines
beginning with \texttt{\#} are annotations; every other line is the literal prompt string, with
the long single-line strings wrapped to fit the page and blank lines marking the paragraph breaks
inside a string. Braced tokens such as \texttt{\{headline\}} are filled per generation.

\begin{verbatim}
# BRIEF: control mode sends only this
Write a news article with the following headline:

{headline}

Aim for about {target_words} words.

# VOICE: prepended to every non-control mode
Act as a professional news journalist. Provide the article a seasoned
staff reporter would write: concrete and specific, in a natural human
voice, never flat or mechanical.

# TAIL: appended to every non-control mode
Keep that natural, human quality all the way through, including the
closing paragraphs.

# PLAN: plan-mode body, between VOICE and BRIEF
Before you write, plan the article the way a reporter does: pick the
lede (the single most important fact), then lay out the who, what,
when, where, why, and how, and the order to present them, most
important first. Work that outline out for yourself, then write the
article to it. Do not show your planning; return only the finished
article.

# FEWSHOT_LEAD / FEWSHOT_TAIL: wrap the exemplar block
Study the voice, pacing, and rhythm of the examples below, then write
in the same spirit. Borrow only their style, never their facts or
wording.

<examples>
<example>
# exemplar 1 (full article text omitted; see "Few-shot exemplars" below)
</example>
# ... two more <example> blocks, three exemplars in total ...

</examples>

# FEEDBACK: self-refine call 2, over call 1's {draft}
You are a demanding editor. Critique the draft below only on how
naturally human its prose reads: its rhythm, pacing, and flow. Name
at least THREE specific passages that read as flat, monotonous, or
mechanical. For each, quote the exact sentence(s) and say concretely
what to change to give it a more human cadence. Do not rewrite the
draft; give only the critique. Even if the writing is already strong,
still name the three weakest spots.

DRAFT:
{draft}

# REWRITE: self-refine call 3, over {draft} and {feedback}
Here is your draft and an editor's critique.

DRAFT:
{draft}

CRITIQUE:
{feedback}

Rewrite the article in full, applying every point of the critique. This
is a substantial rewrite, not a light edit: change how the sentences
move wherever the critique flagged flatness, while preserving the
meaning, the facts, and the target length. Return only the rewritten
article.
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{How the modes compose.} The control mode sends the bare brief and nothing else. Every
other mode follows one template: the voice preamble, then a mode-specific body, then the same
brief, then the tail. The plain instruction has an empty body, so it reduces to the voice
preamble, the brief, and the tail. The plan mode puts the plan text in the body slot, and the
few-shot mode puts the exemplar block there. Self-refine is three model calls on a single
article: a draft from the instruction prompt, a critique from the feedback prompt applied to that
draft, and a rewrite from the rewrite prompt applied to the draft and the critique. A self-check
in \path{bench/prompts.py} asserts the three calls.

\paragraph{Length brief.} The \texttt{\{target\_words\}} slot is the paired human article's word
count rounded to the nearest $25$, so every generation targets its human counterpart's length
(Section~\ref{sec:corpus}).

\paragraph{Few-shot exemplars.} The exemplar block holds three straight news reports drawn from
Wikinews articles outside the $200$ evaluation set. They are the reports whose burstiness sits
nearest the median of the human distribution, chosen for register and topic. The selection uses
$B_n$ only to locate that median and drop degenerate tails, never to pick high-dispersion
exemplars, so the few-shot condition sees typical human rhythm and no exemplar is chosen to teach
to the test. Their full texts are
committed with the released code and are omitted here for length.

\paragraph{Metric-free wording.} None of the prompts names the axis the benchmark scores. A
self-check enforces that no prompt string contains a measured-axis term, among them
\emph{burstiness}, \emph{sentence length}, \emph{variance}, and \emph{standard deviation}. No
prompt gives the model a numeric target on a measured axis; the word count in the length brief is
a length target only. Keeping the axis out of the wording guards against teaching to
the test.

\paragraph{Instruction paraphrases (wording-robustness probe).} The probe in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} re-runs the instruction mode on DeepSeek V4 Pro under three
independent rewrites of the voice and tail above, one seed over all $200$ briefs, each keeping the
persona frame and the metric-free property. The harness is
\path{experiments/ablation/prompt_paraphrase.py}. The three pairs follow, the voice prepended and
the tail appended around the same brief.

\begin{verbatim}
# PARAPHRASE v1 (VOICE then TAIL)
You are a veteran newspaper reporter. Write the story the way
an experienced newsroom professional would: specific, grounded
in facts, with a voice that reads as unmistakably human rather
than machine-written.

Hold that authentic reporter's voice through to the final sentence.

# PARAPHRASE v2 (VOICE then TAIL)
Take the role of a seasoned journalist at a major daily. Produce
the kind of article an editor would run with light touches only:
concrete details, a living, natural voice, nothing stiff or
formulaic.

Make sure the writing still sounds fully human in the closing
paragraphs, not just the opening.

# PARAPHRASE v3 (VOICE then TAIL)
Write as a long-time staff correspondent. The article should
carry the texture of real human reportage: precise facts,
natural phrasing, and none of the flat, mechanical quality of
generated text.

Sustain that human texture to the very end of the piece.
\end{verbatim}

\section{Reference implementation}
\label{sec:refimpl}

The measurement instrument is model-free and pure standard library. The listing below is the
complete, self-contained implementation used in this paper: the sentence segmenter, the
burstiness coefficient $B_n$ (Goh--Barab\'asi under the Kim--Jo finite-$n$ correction), the
memory coefficient $M$, the Gini robustness check, Miller--Madow segmentation entropy, the
multiscale profile, and the Goodhart guard, whose thresholds are its default keyword arguments.
It runs on any Python~3 interpreter with no third-party dependencies and no reference model.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
"""Reference implementation of the burstiness measurement instrument.

Model-free and pure standard library. Segments a document into sentence
lengths and computes the burstiness coefficient B_n (Goh-Barabasi under the
Kim-Jo finite-n correction), the memory coefficient M, the Gini robustness
check, Miller-Madow segmentation entropy, and a multiscale profile. The
Goodhart guard screens degenerate text that would game a dispersion score.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import math
import re
from statistics import fmean, pstdev
from typing import List, Optional, Sequence

# Below this many sentences, B_n and M estimates are noisy and biased; the
# entry point reports ``reliable = False`` rather than trusting them.
RELIABILITY_FLOOR = 10

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Segmentation: sentence lengths in whitespace tokens (stdlib only)
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

_ABBREVIATIONS = {
    "mr", "mrs", "ms", "dr", "prof", "sr", "jr", "st", "vs", "etc",
    "e.g", "i.e", "fig", "no", "vol", "cf", "al", "inc", "ltd", "co",
}
_SENT_SPLIT = re.compile(r'([.!?]+["\')\]]?)\s+')
_PUNCT_SYMBOLS = re.compile(r'[.!?,;:\-—()"\']')


def sentence_lengths(text: str) -> List[int]:
    """Word-token count of each sentence, with an abbreviation-guarded split."""
    if not text or not text.strip():
        return []
    sentences: List[str] = []
    last = 0
    for m in _SENT_SPLIT.finditer(text):
        end = m.end()
        candidate = text[last:end].strip()
        bare = candidate.rstrip('.!?"\')]').strip()
        last_word = bare.split()[-1].lower() if bare.split() else ""
        if last_word in _ABBREVIATIONS:
            continue
        if candidate:
            sentences.append(candidate)
        last = end
    tail = text[last:].strip()
    if tail:
        sentences.append(tail)
    return [len(s.split()) for s in sentences if s.split()]


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Core coefficients (operate on a sequence of sentence lengths)
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def burstiness_B(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Goh-Barabasi coefficient B = (sigma - mu) / (sigma + mu), bounded [-1, 1]."""
    if len(intervals) < 2:
        return None
    mu = fmean(intervals)
    sigma = pstdev(intervals)
    denom = sigma + mu
    if denom <= 0:
        return None
    return (sigma - mu) / denom


def burstiness_B_corrected(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Kim-Jo finite-n correction B_n; reduces to (r-1)/(r+1) as n -> inf."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 2:
        return None
    mu = fmean(intervals)
    if mu <= 0:
        return None
    r = pstdev(intervals) / mu
    sp, sm = math.sqrt(n + 1), math.sqrt(n - 1)
    denom = (sp - 2.0) * r + sm
    if denom == 0:
        return None
    return (sp * r - sm) / denom


def memory_M(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> Optional[float]:
    """Lag-1 autocorrelation of consecutive sentence lengths (the memory term)."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 3:
        return None
    a = list(intervals[:-1])
    b = list(intervals[1:])
    m1, m2 = fmean(a), fmean(b)
    s1, s2 = pstdev(a), pstdev(b)
    if s1 <= 0 or s2 <= 0:
        return None
    cov = fmean((x - m1) * (y - m2) for x, y in zip(a, b))
    return cov / (s1 * s2)


def gini(intervals: Sequence[float]) -> float:
    """Gini coefficient of sentence lengths; scale-free robustness check for B_n."""
    n = len(intervals)
    if n < 2:
        return 0.0
    m = fmean(intervals)
    if m == 0:
        return 0.0
    # Sample (unbiased) Gini: divide by n*(n-1), not n^2, which removes an
    # n-dependent bias that would confound cross-document comparison.
    return sum(abs(a - b) for a in intervals for b in intervals) / (2.0 * n * (n - 1) * m)


_TYPO_QUOTES = str.maketrans({"“": '"', "”": '"', "‘": "'", "’": "'"})


def segmentation_entropy(text: str) -> float:
    """Miller-Madow-corrected Shannon entropy (bits) of the punctuation marks.

    Typographic quotes are normalized to ASCII first, so quote STYLE (curly vs
    straight) is not scored as segmentation diversity.
    """
    counts: dict = {}
    for ch in _PUNCT_SYMBOLS.findall((text or "").translate(_TYPO_QUOTES)):
        counts[ch] = counts.get(ch, 0) + 1
    total = sum(counts.values())
    if total == 0:
        return 0.0
    h = -sum((c / total) * math.log2(c / total) for c in counts.values())
    return h + (len(counts) - 1) / (2.0 * total * math.log(2))


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Multiscale temporal profile
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def _coarse_grain(L: Sequence[float], w: int) -> List[float]:
    """Non-overlapping window means: coarsen the length series to scale w."""
    return [fmean(L[i:i + w]) for i in range(0, len(L) - w + 1, w)]


def multiscale_burstiness(text: str, scales: Sequence[int] = (1, 2, 4)) -> dict:
    """B_n at the sentence scale and at coarse-grained window scales.

    Genuine long-range burstiness holds its dispersion at coarse scales, whereas
    mechanical alternation washes out toward B_n = -1 at scale >= 2.
    """
    L = [float(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    out = {}
    for s in scales:
        cg = list(L) if s == 1 else _coarse_grain(L, s)
        out[f"B_n@{s}"] = burstiness_B_corrected(cg) if len(cg) >= 2 else None
    return out


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Goodhart guard: reject degenerate texts that reach the score by gaming it
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def _trigram_diversity(text: str) -> float:
    """Distinct word-trigram ratio (catches verbatim repetition)."""
    w = (text or "").split()
    if len(w) < 4:
        return 1.0
    tris = list(zip(w, w[1:], w[2:]))
    return len(set(tris)) / len(tris)


def _runon_ratio(text: str) -> float:
    """Internal clause delimiters (, ; :) per sentence terminal (. ! ?)."""
    internal = len(re.findall(r"[,;:]", text or ""))
    terminal = len(re.findall(r"[.!?]+", text or "")) or 1
    return internal / terminal


def gaming_flags(text, *, loo_delta=0.25, m_floor=-0.5, m_ceiling=0.7,
                 distinct_len_min=0.5, trigram_min=0.5, frag_frac_max=0.3,
                 punct_rate_max=0.25, runon_max=8.0, finescale_drop=0.4):
    """Flag texts that reach a high dispersion score through degenerate patterns.

    Returns {"ok", "gate_ok", "flags", "detail"}. ``ok`` means no gaming pattern
    fired; ``gate_ok`` means ok AND reliable (n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR). B_n should
    be counted only when gate_ok is True and a separate coherence gate also
    passes: re-punctuating a fixed text can swing B_n across much of its range,
    so length-rhythm must never select generations on its own.

    Flags: outlier_driven (dropping the longest sentence swings B_n > loo_delta),
    low_len_diversity (distinct-length ratio < distinct_len_min at n >= 8, which
    catches blocky and period-k patterns), repetitive (trigram ratio <
    trigram_min), alternating (M < m_floor), over_clumped (M > m_ceiling),
    fine_scale_only (B_n@1 > 0 but drops by > finescale_drop at scale 2),
    fragment_spam (>= frag_frac_max of sentences under 3 words), punct_gamed
    (punctuation marks per word > punct_rate_max), runon (internal-to-terminal
    delimiter ratio > runon_max).
    """
    Li = [int(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    L = [float(x) for x in Li]
    n = len(L)
    if n < 3:
        return {"ok": False, "gate_ok": False, "flags": {"too_short": True}, "detail": {"n": n}}

    B_full = burstiness_B_corrected(L)
    j = max(range(n), key=lambda i: L[i])
    B_loo = burstiness_B_corrected(L[:j] + L[j + 1:])
    loo = abs(B_full - B_loo) if (B_full is not None and B_loo is not None) else 0.0
    M = memory_M(L)
    distinct_ratio = len(set(Li)) / n
    trig = _trigram_diversity(text)
    frag_frac = sum(1 for x in Li if x < 3) / n
    words = sum(L) or 1.0
    punct_rate = len(_PUNCT_SYMBOLS.findall(text or "")) / words
    runon = _runon_ratio(text)
    ms = multiscale_burstiness(text)
    b1, b2 = ms.get("B_n@1"), ms.get("B_n@2")
    fine_scale_only = (b1 is not None and b2 is not None and b1 > 0.0
                       and (b1 - b2) > finescale_drop)

    flags = {
        "outlier_driven": loo > loo_delta,
        "low_len_diversity": n >= 8 and distinct_ratio < distinct_len_min,
        "repetitive": trig < trigram_min,
        "alternating": M is not None and M < m_floor,
        "over_clumped": M is not None and M > m_ceiling,
        "fine_scale_only": fine_scale_only,
        "fragment_spam": frag_frac >= frag_frac_max,
        "punct_gamed": punct_rate > punct_rate_max,
        "runon": runon > runon_max,
    }
    ok = not any(flags.values())
    return {
        "ok": ok,
        "gate_ok": ok and n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR,
        "flags": flags,
        "detail": {"n": n, "loo_dB": round(loo, 3),
                   "M": None if M is None else round(M, 3),
                   "distinct_ratio": round(distinct_ratio, 3), "trigram_div": round(trig, 3),
                   "frag_frac": round(frag_frac, 3), "punct_rate": round(punct_rate, 3),
                   "runon": round(runon, 2)},
    }


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point: the full model-free vector for a document
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------

def burstiness_pp(text: str) -> dict:
    """Model-free structural burstiness of a document.

    B_n is the primary dispersion coefficient, M the lag-1 memory, Gini the
    robustness check, and multiscale the temporal profile. Segmentation entropy
    is a separate axis (see segmentation_entropy) and surprisal is kept outside
    the construct. ``reliable`` is False below RELIABILITY_FLOOR sentences.
    """
    L = [float(x) for x in sentence_lengths(text)]
    n = len(L)
    return {
        "B_n": burstiness_B_corrected(L),
        "M": memory_M(L),
        "gini": gini(L),
        "multiscale": multiscale_burstiness(text),
        "n_sentences": n,
        "reliable": n >= RELIABILITY_FLOOR,
        "sentence_lengths": [int(x) for x in L],
    }
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Protocol and audit detail}\label{sec:protocoldetail}

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 293--300
\subsection{Corpus parse mechanics}
The human side of each pair is the plain article body, regenerable from the source Wikinews
wikitext by a deterministic MediaWiki parse (\path{experiments/ablation/clean_wikinews.py})
that keeps the body up to the first section heading or reader-comment block, removes templates,
drops reference tags with their contents, resolves wikilinks to their display text, and unwraps
inline formatting. On a held-out check of $100$ articles this parse reproduces the shipped
human bodies exactly.

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 315--319 and 402--404; refusal counts from 405--412
\subsection{Per-leg completeness}
Completeness is disclosed per leg. Of Qwen3.6-27B's $3{,}000$ cells ($200$ headlines $\times$
$5$ modes $\times$ $3$ seeds), $2{,}996$ completed; we excluded the four that exceeded the
reasoning budget rather than scoring their incomplete output. We average affected headlines
over their remaining seeds, so every mode retains $200$ matched pairs. Grok 4.5 reports two
empty self-refine outputs, truncations by signature, seed-averaged away. Provider content
moderation removes cells on two legs, by two different mechanisms. Moonshot's filter refuses
explicitly: $25$ of Kimi K2.6's $3{,}000$ cells return HTTP $400$, one Hong Kong protest
headline refused at every mode and seed. Zhipu's filter refuses silently: $36$ of GLM-5.2's
cells return an ordinary HTTP $200$ with empty output, concentrated on the same cluster of
politically sensitive headlines. The remaining five legs generated all $200$ headlines in
every mode.

% Provenance: paper/tae body 4.4, moved at integration 2026-08-17; source paper/main.tex lines 388--400
\subsection{Per-leg decoding and output caps}
GLM-5.2 ignores a requested temperature and Kimi K2.6 rejects one; Grok 4.5 exposes neither
temperature nor top-$p$; Gemini 3.1 Pro ran at temperature $1.0$ with provider-managed thinking;
Claude Opus 4.8 exposes no temperature. Gemini's thoughts are never returned, and Gemma 3 has
no thinking mode. Anthropic bills reasoning against the output cap. The
shared $16{,}000$-token cap that leaves other families room to write leaves Claude Opus 4.8 with
none at maximum effort: the self-refine rewrite spends the whole budget reasoning. We ran that
leg at high effort with a $32{,}000$-token cap, which restores a comparable prose budget rather
than granting a larger one and matches Gemini 3.1 Pro's thinking level. The leg completed all
$3{,}000$ cells with no truncated or empty output.

% Provenance: paper/tae body 4.3, moved at integration 2026-08-17
\subsection{Confidence intervals}
CIs on median paired deltas are percentile bootstrap intervals: the $2.5$th and $97.5$th
percentiles of the medians of $2{,}000$ resamples of the $200$ matched pairs. Per-seed $B_n$
effect sizes are released alongside the evaluation artifacts and are consistent with the
seed-averaged results.

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 325--329
\subsection{Scaffolding rates}
A generation-hygiene guard flagged markdown scaffolding and echoed headline blocks in every
family we benchmark, from 23--38\% of control-mode generations on one leg (DeepSeek V4 Pro:
23\% markdown, 38\% title lines) to 100\% on another (Gemma 3 27B markdown; headline echoes on
the Grok 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro legs). All of it is formatting the plain-prose human baseline
lacks.

% Provenance: paper/main.tex lines 680--688
\subsection{Contamination probe values}
An $n$-gram contamination probe over $600$ control-mode generations per model on three families
finds mean matched 8-gram overlap with the paired article of $0.00055$ (Qwen3.6-27B, max
$0.036$), $0.00207$ (DeepSeek V4 Pro, max $0.104$), and $0.00098$ (Grok 4.3, max $0.062$),
against a mismatched-article baseline mean of $0.0$ everywhere; 13-gram means are at or below
$0.00064$ for all three. The worst single generation shares roughly $10\%$ of its 8-grams with
its paired article, consistent with a shared quotation rather than memorization. Verbatim
reproduction therefore cannot explain the measured gaps on the three probed families, which
span open-weights and API-served models. The probe covered three of the nine families, on the
control mode only.
